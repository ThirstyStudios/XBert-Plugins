# Phase 1 — Cowork Skeleton Normalisation

Source: `xbert-plugins/Plans/cowork_plugin_{2..6}_*_skeleton.docx`. Plugin 1 (Workflow Review) is excluded — already shipped as `xbert-workflow-review`.

Five plugins normalised below, followed by a consolidated workflow row table for the Phase 2 alignment auditor.

## cowork-#2 — Practice Health Check

- **Slug**: xbert-practice-health-check
- **Audience**: Bookkeepers (most often — single-client drill-down), Practice managers (portfolio triage), Practice owners (portfolio summary). Cohort: established customers (3+ months); degrades gracefully for newer customers (snapshot only, no trend analysis).
- **Trigger**: Periodic / ad-hoc portfolio review. Bookkeepers reach for it when working a specific client and wanting interpretation of the score; managers/owners reach for it to triage attention across the book.
- **Inputs**: One row per (Client × Data Quality Check) with current + prior-period scores. Fields: Client identity (ClientTenantId, ClientName, ClientCode, ClientCountry, AccountingType, IsLocked, IsMarkedForDeletion); Check identity (CheckId, CheckName, CheckCategory, CheckSeverity); Score/result (CurrentScore, CurrentStatus pass/fail/warning, ItemsFlagged, ItemsResolved); Trend data (ScoreLastMonth, ScoreLast3Months, ScoreLast12Months); Context (LastRunTime, DataSourcePlatform Xero/MYOB/etc, ClientPlanType).
- **Deliverable**: Word document (.docx) primary; PDF and markdown secondary. Structure: first-page portfolio summary; Section 1 portfolio snapshot; Section 2 clients needing attention (ranked); Section 3 trending (improving + deteriorating); Section 4 issues by category; Section 5 per-client drill-down (top N flagged, default 10); Section 6 prioritised recommendations.
- **Frequency**: Ad-hoc / periodic (not fixed cadence in brief).
- **Candidate MCP tools**: `Data_DataQualityScore`, `Data_XBertNotificationSummary`, `Data_GetAllAccessibleClients`, `Practice_GetConnectAccountReview`, `Utility_GenerateWord`.
- **Routing Intelligence agent**: PracticeAgent (Connect portal / cross-client portfolio diagnostic) with DataAgent fallback for client-level metrics.
- **Capability gaps**: Existing client health check report described as "descriptive stats only" — diagnostic + prescriptive interpretation layer doesn't exist yet. Trend storage at the per-check granularity is unconfirmed (whether historical scores are stored, and at what granularity, is an open question to share when specced in detail). Cross-accounting-system coverage variance (richer for Xero than FreeAgent) — needs "check not supported on your accounting system" handling rather than blank cells.
- **Open questions** (from brief): Does drill-down belong in same document or as separate per-client docs (50-client practice would produce huge single doc)? Are there any existing in-app screens already showing cross-client data quality comparison (would narrow non-overlap argument)?
- **Workshop insights** (anonymised): Addresses low-adoption churn theme (~40% of meaningful cancellations) by making existing XBert data useful via interpretation/recommendations; speaks to mature-user cohort (median 634 day tenure) waiting for advisory features; gives practice owners/managers visibility they don't currently have.
- **Non-overlap argument**: Existing client health check is single-client and descriptive only; this is portfolio-level + diagnostic + prescriptive — cross-client comparison is the load-bearing capability practice managers and owners don't have today.

## cowork-#3 — End-of-Period Dashboard

- **Slug**: xbert-end-of-period-dashboard
- **Audience**: Bookkeepers (checking their own clients are ready), Practice managers (overall readiness, identifying clients at risk), Practice owners (higher-level view, particularly year-end/quarterly compliance). Cohort: established customers, AU-only for v1 (multi-jurisdiction is v2).
- **Trigger**: Period boundary — month-end, quarter-end, year-end. Single plugin with three views (month, quarter, year). Period-close is a natural revisit moment that re-engages customers who haven't logged in for months.
- **Inputs**: Most data-heavy of the five. Fuses three sources: (1) Workflow data — tasks, status, outstanding work; (2) Data quality data — flagged issues; (3) Compliance obligation data — BAS, IAS, STP finalisation, tax returns per client per period; (4) Calendar / lodgement data — due dates from ATO + other regulators. Row shape: one row per (Client × Obligation × Period) with status, due date, related XBert work, related data quality items (denormalised wide). Possibly a second extract for non-obligation-driven workflow tasks and general period data quality issues.
- **Deliverable**: Word document primary, but flagged for workshop: HTML/interactive may be more appropriate if the user refers back repeatedly through the period (vs reading once at period-start). Structure: first-page summary ("X of Y clients ready; Z lodgements due in next N days; W items needing attention"); Section 1 period overview; Section 2 per-client readiness; Section 3 compliance lodgements due; Section 4 outstanding work; Section 5 data quality issues affecting period close; Section 6 prioritised actions.
- **Frequency**: Monthly / quarterly / annual (three views in one plugin).
- **Candidate MCP tools**: `Data_GetAllAccessibleClients`, `Data_DataQualityScore`, `Data_XBertNotificationSummary`, `Features_Review`, `Features_GetActualTimeList`, `Data_XPMClients`, `Data_XPMTimesheets_*`, `Validation_BASReconciliation`, `Validation_VATReconciliation`, `Validation_PayrollReconciliation`, `Data_LockDates`, `Data_ReconciliationStatus`, `Utility_GenerateWord`.
- **Routing Intelligence agent**: PracticeAgent (portfolio-level period readiness) with FeaturesAgent (work / capacity / outstanding tasks) and DataAgent (financial obligation data) as supporting agents.
- **Capability gaps**: Compliance obligation data (BAS/IAS/STP/tax-return statuses per client per period) — unconfirmed whether this exists in XBert today or needs to be derived. ATO lodgement-date calendar / external regulator due-date source is not in the MCP tool inventory. AU-only constraint for v1 limits cross-jurisdiction practices.
- **Open questions** (from brief): Does compliance obligation data exist in XBert today or need derivation? Word vs HTML/interactive deliverable? Any existing XBert reports already doing compliance-period summarisation (would narrow non-overlap to "user-customisable + drill-down")?
- **Workshop insights** (anonymised): Hits low-adoption churn theme by serving the highest-pressure moment in the bookkeeping calendar; addresses "set this up once and never came back to it" cohort (period close is a natural re-engagement moment); positions XBert against Karbon as the workflow tool of record rather than something competitors absorb. Jack's input pending on what AU practices actually need at period end.
- **Non-overlap argument**: XBert work screens show individual tasks but don't fuse with compliance obligations and data quality at period level. Accounting systems show lodgement obligations but not workflow status. XPM has practice-management data but not the data quality / workflow exception layer. The fusion is the only place all three data sources come together in a period-close-shaped view.

## cowork-#4 — Bank Statement Upload

- **Slug**: xbert-bank-statement-upload
- **Audience**: Bookkeepers, primarily — hands-on file-transformation work the bookkeeper does themselves. Practice managers indirect (care about team time savings). Cohort: all tenures, but disproportionately valuable for new and growing practices and a strong early-tenure activation candidate (first-client onboarding "saved me 4 hours" experience).
- **Trigger**: New-client onboarding moment when bookkeeper must bring multiple historical bank accounts up to date (also any ad-hoc need to backfill historical statements in irregular formats that the bank feed can't handle).
- **Inputs**: Structurally different from the other 4 — primary input is uploaded files, not an MCP extract. MCP data still needed for: Client identity (which client, which accounting system, which bank account(s)); Accounting system upload format spec (Xero CSV format differs from MYOB, QBO differs again). Uploaded files: PDF bank statements (scanned or text-PDF, many bank-specific layouts), CSV/TSV (varying column orders, header names, date formats), OFX/QIF (structured), Excel (less common), other formats as discovered. Critical design point: plugin can't know in advance what a bank's PDF looks like — needs robust extraction across many layouts AND a confirm/correct human-review step for low-confidence extractions.
- **Deliverable**: A CSV (or whatever the target accounting system requires) ready for upload, plus a markdown audit document showing what was extracted, what was uncertain, and any rows that needed human resolution. Audit document is important for bookkeeper verification and practice compliance trail.
- **Frequency**: Ad-hoc / event-driven (per new-client onboarding, ~3-5 bank accounts per client).
- **Candidate MCP tools**: `Data_BankAccounts`, `Data_BankStatements`, `SemanticExtraction_ExtractText`, `SemanticExtraction_ProcessLineItems`, `Utility_GenerateExcel` (for CSV output via Excel pipeline if applicable), plus accounting-system upload-format metadata (no obvious tool in current inventory).
- **Routing Intelligence agent**: DataAgent (bank account / statement context) with heavy reliance on SemanticExtraction tooling (PDF parsing, line-item extraction). Realistically may need a dedicated handler — closest existing affinity is the receipt-capture-style document extraction pipeline (ReceiptCaptureAgent pattern) extended to bank statements.
- **Capability gaps**: PDF extraction confidence and human-in-the-loop "preview and confirm" workflow are not first-class capabilities of the existing plugin/agent surface; need to be designed. Per-accounting-system upload format spec (Xero/MYOB/QBO CSV column shape) is not in the MCP tool inventory — likely needs a new reference data source. No existing MCP tool covers "validated CSV ready for upload to target accounting system." Sandbox-style multi-format file ingestion (PDF/CSV/TSV/OFX/QIF/Excel) is not currently a plugin capability.
- **Open questions** (from brief): Would bookkeeper tolerate a "preview and confirm" step before final output, or wants it produced and downloaded in one shot? Examples of bank statement files in different formats and the actual upload format specs for Xero/MYOB/QBO bank-statement imports still need to be shared for detailed spec.
- **Workshop insights** (anonymised): Addresses "no time to onboard" (meta-onboarding pain — file transformation friction blocks the bookkeeper from onboarding XBert itself), Cost/ROI (concrete measurable time saving per client onboarded — 20-40 minutes per account × 3-5 accounts per client = 1-3 hours per new client), and 0-90 day churn (early-stage customers see value immediately before forming "paying for this and not using it" judgement).
- **Non-overlap argument**: XBert does not do bank statement transformation today (confirmed). Accounting systems' own bank-feed setup is painful for historical statements and irregular formats. Generic OCR / PDF-to-CSV tools don't know about target-accounting-system formats — the plugin's edge is producing output specifically shaped for upload. Probably the cleanest non-overlap of the five.

## cowork-#5 — Trial Balance Alignment

- **Slug**: xbert-trial-balance-alignment
- **Audience**: Bookkeepers, primarily — they receive the accountant's financials and do the alignment work today; they run the plugin and review the output. Accountants may be secondary readers wanting visibility into journals being posted from their financials. Cohort: established customers; trigger is event-driven.
- **Trigger**: Event-driven — the moment the accountant sends back financials, typically year-end but can be more frequent. Workshop signal: a single mid-sized practice has 12 entities × 2-3 years of adjustments = 30-40 alignments per year, ranging 2-3 hours (simple) to 6-10+ hours (complex).
- **Inputs**: Two inputs, both required. **(1) Accounting system trial balance** — v1 Xero: accepts standard Xero TB export (CSV or Excel) directly, no MCP query required for v1; required columns Account Code, Account, Account Type, Debit YTD, Credit YTD (Net column excluded from output). v2+ MYOB/QBO/FreeAgent: either accept their native TB exports or pull via MCP if accounting system's data is already in XBert (decision deferred). **(2) Accountant's financial statements** — uploaded by bookkeeper as PDF (most common), Excel (possible), Word (possible), other formats as discovered. Plugin extracts figures from the document; format variance is high (CCH, MYOB AE, APS, XPM Reports, custom Word templates) so plugin needs robust extraction OR confirm/correct human review. Matching uses Account Code (when present, most reliable), Account Name, and Account Type as criteria.
- **Deliverable**: Two outputs. **(1) Excel comparative-review workbook** — file name `YYYYMMDD-[ClientName]-TB-comparative-review.xlsx`. Sheet 1 "Comparative Review": columns Account Code, Account, Account Type, TB Debit YTD, TB Credit YTD, FS Debit, FS Credit, Variance Debit, Variance Credit, Flag, Notes; variance columns formula-driven (= TB - FS); Flag column shows "REVIEW" for any non-zero variance; rows grouped by Account Type with section subtotals; Grand Total row + three Balance Check rows verifying TB, FS, and Variance all net to zero (zero-tolerance — any non-zero balance gets bold red "IMBALANCE"). Sheet 2 "FS Mapping Log": FS Line Item, FS Value, Mapped TB Account(s), Match Type (ONE-TO-ONE / CONSOLIDATED / UNMATCHED-TB / UNMATCHED-FS), Notes including PDF extraction confidence flags. **(2) Word audit document** — circulatable summary covering what the TB was, what the FS showed, where they aligned, where they differed (with interpretation), the proposed journal(s) for each material variance in draft form, and any items needing human resolution. The plugin produces journal recommendations only — it does not post journals; the bookkeeper enters them in the accounting system manually.
- **Frequency**: Ad-hoc / event-driven, typically annual at year-end (occasionally more frequent). 30-40 per year for a mid-sized practice.
- **Candidate MCP tools**: `Data_TrialBalance`, `Data_BalanceSheet`, `Data_FinancialStatements`, `Data_JournalMaster_*`, `Data_ManualJournals_*`, `SemanticExtraction_ExtractText`, `SemanticExtraction_ProcessLineItems`, `Utility_GenerateExcel`, `Utility_GenerateWord`. (v1 Xero upload path largely sidesteps `Data_TrialBalance` — user uploads the CSV — so for v1 the heavy lifting is in SemanticExtraction + Utility_GenerateExcel/Word.)
- **Routing Intelligence agent**: DataAgent (TB, financial statements, journals) with strong SemanticExtraction dependency (parsing accountant PDFs). UtilityAgent for Word/Excel output generation.
- **Capability gaps**: PDF extraction confidence and human-in-the-loop confirm/correct workflow (same gap as cowork-#4); plugin needs to flag low-confidence extractions in the Notes column rather than guessing. Account-name fuzzy matching strategy when accountant's names don't match TB's ("Office Expenses" vs "Office Costs") — exact/fuzzy/code-based/human-review approach not yet decided. Journal-upload-file output (vs manual entry) is out of v1 scope but is a future capability. Multi-year backlog handling (year-by-year vs multi-year-at-once) affects UX and needs design.
- **Open questions** (from brief): v1 = Xero only confirmed? Multi-accountant format variance (one accountant per client always same format, or multiple varying)? Account-name matching strategy? Material vs immaterial differences — auto-journal small differences under a configurable threshold? Journal-upload-file output for v2+? Backlog handling year-by-year vs multi-year?
- **Workshop insights** (anonymised): Bookkeeper reports 30-40 alignments per year for a single mid-sized practice (12 entities × 2-3 years of adjustments), 2-3 hours (simple) to 6-10+ hours (complex) = 80-300+ hours per year per practice at risk for automation, tens of thousands of dollars cost-of-time per practice per year. One of the strongest activation candidates in the top 5. Hits low-adoption (daily-tool activation), product gaps / poor support (mature-user advisory wait), and Cost/ROI (defensible time saving) churn themes.
- **Non-overlap argument**: XBert does not do trial balance reconciliation against external documents today. Accounting systems can show their own TB but can't reconcile it against a PDF or external document. Bookkeeper's current process is manual matching in Excel or on paper. Possibly the cleanest non-overlap of the five alongside Bank Statement Upload.

## cowork-#6 — Client Fee Review

- **Slug**: xbert-client-fee-review
- **Audience**: Practice owners, primarily — fee decisions belong at the top of the practice. Practice managers as secondary users (often run the review process even when owner signs off). Senior bookkeepers in larger practices where pricing is delegated. Cohort: established customers (3+ months) with meaningful XBert and XPM data history; should degrade gracefully (explicit "insufficient data" list rather than low-confidence recommendations).
- **Trigger**: Annual (or ad-hoc) client fee review across whole client portfolio. "Hell yes, would use immediately" workshop signal from a practice owner.
- **Inputs**: **XBert workflow data** — actual time logged per client by service / task category (same data Workflow Review uses for budget-vs-actual, rolled up to client-fee-relevant aggregates). **XBert transaction volume data** — monthly counts per client across six categories from Cass Scott strategic-pricing brief (bank transactions, AP documents, AR documents, payroll runs as pay events not employees, expense claims, manual journals). **XPM data** — current fees per client, scope statements / engagement letters if accessible, billing history. **Optional but valuable** — practice cost-base / floor-rate data from strategic-pricing model (if they have one) for margin calculations; without it, plugin falls back to time-based proxies. Row shape: one row per (Client × Month × Service Category), 12 months minimum to detect trends, 24+ months ideal.
- **Deliverable**: Word document primary, with optional Excel companion containing underlying detail data (for practices that want to do their own analysis on top). Structure: first-page portfolio summary (recovery position, counts, total exposure); Section 1 clients flagged for fee increase (ranked by gap); Section 2 clients confirmed appropriately priced (the "no change" evidence); Section 3 clients flagged for fee decrease; Section 4 clients with insufficient data; Section 5 per-client drill-down (one page per flagged client); Section 6 how to use this in an annual client review (practical notes on having the conversation). The plugin does not produce client-facing proposals — those belong downstream (Cass's strategic-pricing tool or a future plugin).
- **Frequency**: Annual primary (with ad-hoc support).
- **Candidate MCP tools**: `Features_GetActualTimeList`, `Features_Review` (capacity), `Data_XPMTimesheets_*`, `Data_XPMClients`, `Data_BankTransactions_*`, `Data_Bills_*`, `Data_Invoices_*`, `Data_PayRuns_*`, `Data_Payslips_*`, `Data_ManualJournals_*`, `Data_GetAllAccessibleClients`, `Utility_GenerateWord`, `Utility_GenerateExcel`.
- **Routing Intelligence agent**: FeaturesAgent (time logged, capacity, work-management aggregates) as primary, with DataAgent (transaction volumes across the six pricing categories) and PracticeAgent (portfolio-level recovery view) as supporting.
- **Capability gaps**: Aggregation of XBert time logged "per client per service category" may need new derivation (open question from brief: whether XBert currently aggregates at this grain or if new derivation is needed). XPM scope-statement / engagement-letter access depth is unconfirmed — needed to map fee changes back to engagement-letter context. Cost-base / floor-rate data ingestion is optional but not currently a first-class plugin input; without it the plugin falls back to time-based proxies (weaker margin evidence). Multiple pricing-basis types (time/volume/value/hybrid) — practice is "moving from hourly to other metrics" — v1 scope across these is undecided. In-scope vs out-of-scope work detection mechanism not yet defined.
- **Open questions** (from brief): Commercial relationship with Cass's Strategic Pricing Tool (evidence engine vs full modelling tool — which framing wins?). Pricing model variance across work types (separate vs aggregated assessment). Multiple pricing-basis support in v1 (time/volume/value/hybrid). In-scope vs out-of-scope work detection. Confidence framing in output (qualitative bands vs numeric scores — probably qualitative for readability). Engagement-letter integration. Cohort-defining minimum data history (provisional 12 months).
- **Workshop insights** (anonymised): "Hell yes, would use immediately" from a practice owner. Workshop notes pricing differs by work type (consulting / bookkeeping / systems implementation each priced differently); practice is moving from hourly to other metrics (volume, value); key need is in-scope vs out-of-scope work identification and how pricing is articulated around that. Hits Cost/ROI (pricing decisions affect practice bottom line directly), Switched-competitor (Karbon — fee review / pricing intelligence anchors XBert as commercial intelligence tool not just workflow), and Product gaps / poor support (mature-user advisory feature wait).
- **Non-overlap argument**: XBert work screens show individual time logged but don't aggregate to client-fee-relevant comparisons. XPM shows fees and billing but doesn't fuse with cost-to-serve data. Accounting system holds none of this — revenue and cost-of-delivery aren't aggregated at client level by Xero/MYOB/etc. The fusion of workflow time + transaction volume + current fee, aggregated and compared per client, presented as portfolio-level evidence, is the edge. Overlap with Cass's Strategic Pricing Tool is the open commercial question (this plugin is narrower — evidence + recommendation only; Cass's tool covers cost-base modelling, scope determination, proposal generation, ongoing monitoring).
