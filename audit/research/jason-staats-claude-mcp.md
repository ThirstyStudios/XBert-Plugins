## 3. Recurring themes (patterns he keeps coming back to)

- **Claude (Anthropic) is the right starting point for firms in 2026** - he explicitly tells accountants to bias to Claude Desktop / Cowork over ChatGPT for accounting work. This is a position shift from his 2024 ChatGPT-led content.
- **Five levels of AI autonomy** is his consistent maturity model: live chat -> saved chat -> scheduled task -> hosted routine -> managed agent. Firms are mostly stuck at L1-L2; the unlock is L4 (Claude hosted routines + webhooks).
- **Cross-system agents beat app-specific AI**. He is openly skeptical of "AI features inside your accounting app" and bullish on tool-agnostic agents (Claude + MCP) that span Karbon + Xero/QBO + Gmail + filesystem.
- **"AI can only help to the degree that your processes are standardised"** - he repeats this in workshops and interviews; AI exposes SOP debt.
- **Bookkeeping is moving from data-entry to oversight + control** - the role of AI is to handle the routine and surface exceptions for human review. (Matches XBert framing exactly.)
- **Month-end close document collection** and **browser automation** are where today agents still break - so they are the most valuable next targets.
- **Firms should own their data and orchestrate flows themselves** (Karbon interview) - he wants accountants doing the integration work, not waiting for vendors.
- **Train AI on firm-specific procedure**, not generic guidance - he uses Skills + project knowledge for this.
- **Adoption needs daily team habits and incentives**, not training - the "Six blockers" episode argues training alone never sticks; Claude has to show up in the daily flow.
- **AI hallucinations on financial data are the unsolved trust problem** - he keeps returning to it; favours systems where the AI works against real source documents (MCP) rather than retyping numbers from chat.
- **MCP / Intuit partnership is a turning point** - he treats the Anthropic-Intuit MCP deal as the moment "real" agentic accounting becomes possible for non-developer firm owners.
- **Receipt capture is solved; receipt-to-ledger matching is not** - he keeps highlighting this gap.

## 4. MCP servers he uses / recommends

- **Intuit QuickBooks Online MCP server** (intuit/quickbooks-online-mcp-server - official) - exposes QBO entities and write actions (journals, classifications, queries) to Claude. Centrepiece of his "Build a Custom AI Accountant" demo. He calls out the Intuit-Anthropic partnership (Feb 2026) as the moment this becomes mainstream.
- **Xero MCP server** (XeroAPI/xero-mcp-server - official from Xero) - bridge between MCP and Xero API. He references it as the Xero-side equivalent of the QBO MCP, but most of his hands-on demos to date are QBO.
- **Filesystem MCP (built-in)** - he uses local-folder MCP access constantly: bank CSVs, chart of accounts, client document folders. Most of the 30-min Cowork guide is this.
- **Claude Cowork connectors** (Gmail, Google Drive, Google Calendar, Microsoft 365) - he treats these as MCP-equivalent integrations for the firm-ops side of the workflow (intake, scheduling, comms).
- **Intuit / Anthropic Claude for Small Business bundle** (Feb 2026 partnership) - 15 prebuilt agent workflows that span QuickBooks + PayPal + HubSpot + Canva + Docusign + Google Workspace + Microsoft 365. He flags this as the off-the-shelf path firms should compare any custom build against.
- **Karbon** - he does not say there is an official MCP yet, but he repeatedly tells firms practice-management is the missing piece for end-to-end agent workflows and treats it as the next must-have MCP target.

## Mapping back to XBert

The three highest-confidence "XBert should have a system agent for this" candidates from his body of work:

1. **Month-end close document collection agent** - he calls this out as the unsolved problem. Maps directly to a Connect/Client-tenant agent that owns the chase list (which docs, which clients, which deadlines) and orchestrates capture + reminder + escalation. XBert already has the data spine (Notifications, Connect/Client hierarchy, Capture for receipts).

2. **Receipt-to-transaction matching agent** - he demoes this manually in the 30-min Cowork guide and explicitly says it should run unattended. XBert existing Receipt Capture + XDI / fact extraction stack is the natural home; gap today is the matching + ledger-write step running as an agent rather than a UI flow.

3. **Multi-entity consolidation agent** (and the closely related workpaper bundler) - one of his "six use cases" and his "AI workpaper automation experiment" topic. Maps to a Custom XBert / system agent at the Connect level that pulls per-client trial balances, eliminates intercompany, and produces a consolidated pack with workpapers - cleanly inside XBert Connect-roll-up model.

Honourable mention: **SOP generator from screen recordings** - this is firm-internal but a strong potential plugin since it solves the "we never document anything" complaint and could feed XBert own knowledge base / agent grounding.
